# Motion Studio site

The project's GitHub Pages site: a home page in `src/` with live Motion Studio compositions from `compositions/`, and a
user guide (`src/guide.html`, served at `/motion-studio/guide.html`) with screenshots of the app. The compositions are
played by the product's own runtime (`packages/composition-runtime`) and checked by its own validator (`packages/shared`).
Deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`.

## Build and preview

```bash
pnpm install --filter "@motion-studio/composition-runtime..."
pnpm --filter @motion-studio/composition-runtime build
node site/build.mjs                 # → site/_site (git-ignored)
node site/scripts/serve.mjs         # → http://127.0.0.1:4400/motion-studio/
node --test 'site/test/*.test.mjs'
```

`build.mjs` fails on any composition the product would reject.

## Compositions

Each folder in `compositions/` is a regular Motion Studio composition (`composition.json` and `assets/`) plus:
- `meta.json`: `format` (a preset id), `posterTime` (seconds) and `label` (the description used as alt text);
- `poster.webp`: the frame at `posterTime`, shown before the runtime is ready, under reduced motion and without JS;
- `subtitles.json` (only with a `subtitles` clip): the subtitle set, times in ms, inlined in the runtime page as the core does.

The site's fonts (`src/assets/fonts/`) are copied into every composition's `assets/fonts/` at build time.

The home tells a story in sections (`index.html`, `css/story.css`, `js/scenes.js`): Motion Studio makes its own launch
video. The hero (`[data-scene="brief"]`) plays step 1, the brief becoming three variants and one chosen; then one
`section.step` per step (`data-scene` names it), each with a step label, a title, two lines of copy and its demo: a small
canvas of the app (`.scene`, its boards and panels placed with `--x/--y/--w/--h` in the scene's own units, `--sw × --sh`,
and `--px/--py/--pw/--ph` in `--psw × --psh` when the canvas is narrow, by a container query; one unit is `--u`, so the
text inside scales with it) beside its chat, drawn as an illustration (bubbles, never an input). Each demo is written in its
finished state: that is the page without JS and under reduced motion. With motion, `scenes.js` gives each demo a GSAP
timeline that replays its step in a loop at its own pace (a hold on the finished state, then again), playing while the
demo is on screen and paused otherwise, from its start after it has been off screen entirely; never tied to the scroll. A
new version wipes in (`data-wipe`) over the poster of the one before (`.board__before`). The
final cut (`figure[data-final-cut]`, `data-autoplay="false"`) is a composition player with controls (`js/final-cut.js`): it never
starts by itself; its big Play starts it from 0:00 with the sound on.

Live runtimes are costly (each is a page, and each of its html layers a frame; on iOS they all share one content process):
the page holds at most two at once on a phone (a touch screen or under 960 px) and four on a computer (`LIVE_BUDGET` in
`js/player.js`). A player past the budget waits on its poster; one that leaves the screen's surroundings is unloaded
(`about:blank`, then removed), never kept paused. The final cut has no runtime until its Play. Each composition has a
half-size poster (`poster-half.webp`, written by `scripts/posters.mjs`, `--half` to redo only those) that phones load.

Nothing speaks unless the viewer asks (`js/sounds.js`): the Generate and Subtitles demos each have a Play with sound
button. Generate's plays the voice-over it made, then a taste of its music bed; Subtitles' lets its board speak its line,
in sync with the subtitles it draws. One sound at a time (the final cut's Play stops a demo's, a demo's pauses the film);
a demo's sound stops when it leaves the screen, and the demo holds its finished state while it plays. The final cut's own
sound switch is `js/sound.js`.

The hero creatives (`kova-*`, `tidefall-*`, `fernwise-*`) and the story's boards (`story-*`, `story.mjs`) are generated:
change `scripts/hero-gen/`, not their JSON.

```bash
node site/build.mjs                         # once: the width measurer uses the built site's fonts
node site/scripts/hero-gen/build.mjs        # → site/compositions/<id>/composition.json, meta.json, assets/*.svg
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/posters.mjs kova-9x16 …   # then the posters of what changed
```

`kova.mjs`, `tidefall.mjs`, `fernwise.mjs` and `story.mjs` lay out each creative per format with the builders of `lib.mjs`. Text
widths come from `widths.json` (measured in Chrome with the site's fonts by `measure.mjs`, which the build runs by itself
for a new string), so generating needs plain node only. A site test regenerates everything and fails on any difference
from the checked-in files.

## The gallery

After Get started, a wall of twelve boards (`index.html`'s `#gallery`, `css/gallery.css`, `js/gallery.js`): nine made-up
brands generated by `scripts/gallery-gen/` (`gallery-*`, one format each) and KOVA 16:9, TIDEFALL 4:5 and Fernwise 9:16.
Change the generator, not their JSON; a site test regenerates them and fails on any difference. `gallery-gen` uses
hero-gen's `lib.mjs` and `widths.json` (and measures new texts the same way), plus `kit.mjs` (letters and words with a
stagger, lines drawn by a wipe along their own direction). Three of the creatives' display faces are not the site's own:
Fraunces, Anton and Caveat, self-hosted in `src/assets/fonts/` with their OFL licences (Fraunces's widths are measured at
their own size, for its optical sizes).

```bash
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/gallery-gen/build.mjs   # → site/compositions/gallery-*
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/posters.mjs gallery-osteria-4x5 …
```

`gallery.js` plays the boards on screen, muted and looping: at most four at once (the most visible), at most six runtimes
mounted; the others, and every board under reduced motion or without JS, show their poster.

## The launch film

`compositions/launch-16x9` (about a minute) is Motion Studio's own launch video: the home page's eleven steps, one beat
each, with the interface redrawn as vector motion, a voice-over line per beat, ending on "Brief in. Motion out.". It is
generated: change `scripts/launch-gen/`, not its JSON (`engine.mjs` keeps the moves of a group, such as a board or a
panel, apart from each element's own and writes their sum per clip; `launch.mjs` is the film, written in authoring
seconds; `timeline.mjs` stretches each beat to its voice-over line and the engine maps every time through it; text widths
come from `hero-gen/widths.json`). A site test regenerates it and fails on any difference.

Its media are made by `scripts/audio-gen/` with the product's own providers and the maintainer's keys
(`~/.config/motion-studio-site/elevenlabs.key` and `openai.key`, read only by that script): the voice-over lines
(`lines.mjs`), timed word by word, the Generate beat's picture, and the music bed and sound effects (ElevenLabs). The voice
is normalised to about -16 LUFS; the music bed is ducked under it in the composition (`musicStretches`). It writes the
files next to the film (`assets/audio/`, `assets/generated-*`) and `media.json` (lengths, words, waveform peaks), which
the generators read. Re-running it keeps what exists; every paid call prints its estimated cost. The byte-identical
tests leave these files out (they come from paid calls).

```bash
node site/scripts/audio-gen/build.mjs                    # what is missing (paid calls, re-runnable)
node site/scripts/audio-gen/build.mjs trial elevenlabs <voice id>…   # line 01 in a few voices, to choose one
```

```bash
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/launch-gen/build.mjs      # → compositions/launch-16x9
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/posters.mjs launch-16x9
packages/core/node_modules/.bin/tsx site/scripts/launch-gen/render.ts <empty temp dir>
```

`render.ts` renders the film with the product's renderer (`packages/core`: composition server, system Chrome through the
core's provider with its isolation flags, ffmpeg, which mixes the audio clips; `max` quality) into the temp folder, then
writes the site's copy `src/assets/video/launch.mp4` (H.264 1080p, about 1.5 Mbit/s, AAC 128 kbit/s, ~11 MB) and `src/assets/video/launch-poster.webp` (the
composition's poster).

After changing a composition, render its poster again (headless Chrome with a throwaway profile):

```bash
node site/build.mjs && node site/scripts/posters.mjs <id>   # no id: all; `og` also writes src/assets/og-image.png
```

## Checks in a real browser

```bash
node site/scripts/check.mjs --out /tmp/site-check
```

At 360, 768, 1440 and 2000 px, with and without reduced motion: no horizontal scroll, no errors or failed requests, every
composition live or on its poster, nothing told to play under reduced motion, every link with its target or file, the
final cut's slot and controls, and no poster a near-blank frame (luminance deviation under 12). With motion, scrolled to
each step, the story shows that step, every board it plays has a runtime, at most 9 runtimes are on the canvas, and none
plays once the story has scrolled away; the story's sound button turns step 6's line and step 9's board voice on, and off again; under reduced motion every step is a static block with its messages and its
result loaded. In the gallery, at most four boards play and six runtimes are mounted at any scroll
position, none plays once scrolled away, none mounts under reduced motion, and every board is live or on its poster. Then the guide at the same widths: no
horizontal scroll, every screenshot loaded and with alt text, every in-page link with its target, the contents collapsed
below 1000 px. Set `CHROME` if Chrome is not in a standard place.

## Guide screenshots

The guide's screenshots (`src/assets/guide/*.webp`, 1440×900 or a panel of it, light theme with two dark ones) are taken
from the real app:

```bash
pnpm install && pnpm build                 # the web UI the demo serves (packages/web/dist)
node site/scripts/guide-shots.mjs          # all of them, about 15 minutes
node site/scripts/guide-shots.mjs --only editor,export   # only these images (every step still runs)
```

`guide-shots.mjs` starts the real core and web UI (`scripts/guide/demo-server.ts`, under the core's `tsx`) on a demo
folder whose path names no user (`/private/tmp/ms-guide-demo`, or `GUIDE_DEMO`), with an in-memory keychain and no
keys, and `scripts/guide/demo-agent.mjs` in place of Claude Code: it speaks Claude Code's stream-json and answers each
job with fixed work, in the brand of the project it runs in (the site's hero creatives KOVA Air, TIDEFALL and Fernwise as
compositions, a brand proposal, a filled brief, a video analysis written from its frames, a permission request). No model,
no paid service and no real configuration is used. It seeds three projects (KOVA, TIDEFALL, Fernwise) through the app's
API and its UI, renders an identity set with the app's own Chrome renderer, seeds a subtitle set with the app's own
writer (`scripts/guide/seed-subtitles.ts`: there is no transcription engine), and drives headless Chrome
(`scripts/chrome.mjs`: throwaway profile, mock keychain) with `scripts/guide/browser.mjs`. The steps are in
`scripts/guide/shots.mjs`, in order: each builds on the state the previous ones left. It needs macOS `say` for the spoken
clips (a tone elsewhere). It never deletes anything: the demo folder must be empty or absent at the start and is left in
place (remove it by hand, or set `GUIDE_DEMO` to a new folder); a failed step leaves `<demo>-failure.webp`. Look at every
image before committing: none may show a path, a name, a key or a token.

## Third parties

None at runtime. GSAP 3.13.0 (and SplitText, unused) is vendored in `src/vendor/gsap/` under its no-charge standard
licence; the fonts (Bricolage Grotesque, Instrument Sans, JetBrains Mono) are self-hosted under the SIL Open Font
License, with their licence files; so are Fraunces, Anton and Caveat, used only by the gallery's compositions.
